The atelier  ·  Lisbon, since 2019

We don’t send bouquets. We send one flower, and everything we know about it.

Florae began as a herbarium in a Lisbon attic: six hundred pressed sheets, a brass microscope, and a list of places the flower trade has never reached.

Today twenty-three growers on five continents cut a single stem for each member on the same morning. It travels in a cedar box with its plate, its coordinates, and a letter from the hands that grew it.

Rosa ‘Explorer’

The equator rose

Origin
Cayambe volcano, Ecuador0°02′ N  ·  2,850 m
Bloom season
All yearthe equator has no seasons, only altitude
Grown by
Rosa QuishpeFinca Yanahurco, third generation

Twelve hours of vertical sun and cold Andean nights grow a head the size of a fist on a stem as straight as a rule. Forty-two petals, velvet to the touch, darkest where they are still furled.

Protea cynaroides

The king that waits for fire

Origin
Kogelberg, Western Cape34°19′ S  ·  400 m, in the fynbos
Bloom season
May — Octoberthe Cape winter, when the rain comes
Grown by
Thandi Mokoenaon land her grandmother burned and replanted
Keeps
For yearsdried on its own stem, out of water

What looks like one flower is two hundred, packed into a cone and guarded by pink, silver-haired bracts. In the wild it waits for fire to open its seed. It arrives fresh and dries without losing its shape.

Phalaenopsis amabilis

The moon orchid

Origin
Crocker Range, Sabah, Borneo5°25′ N  ·  900 m, in the canopy
Bloom season
Spikes in springeach flower stays open up to a hundred days
Grown by
The Kinabalu Orchid Trustfrom nursery seed-stock, never the wild
Arrives
Rooted, in barkfrom the same forest it came from

An epiphyte: it lives in the treetops and never touches soil. Its lip is a landing strip painted for a single carpenter bee. Keep its roots in the light and it will flower for you again next spring.

Morpho peleides

The one that isn’t a flower

Origin
Osa Peninsula, Costa Rica8°32′ N  ·  lowland rainforest
On the wing
All yearmost in the green season, May — November
Reared by
Co-operative of Rancho Quemadotwelve families, farm-bred stock
Arrives
Under museum glassafter a natural life of three weeks

Every twelfth month we send the visitor instead of the visited. There is no blue pigment in these wings: the scales are ridged like tiny shelves that fold light into colour. Watch the blue change as it breathes.
