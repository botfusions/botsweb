01 The walk EVA 1 · rim traverse · 38 m · he walks as fast as you scroll

00 m · Regolith

It isn’t sand.

It’s volcanic glass, shattered by four billion years of impacts and never weathered smooth. It holds a bootprint the way plaster holds a hand.

09 m · 1.62 m/s²

You weigh a sixth. Your mass doesn’t.

Starting is effortless. Stopping takes planning. By the second hour you stop walking and start to lope.

17 m · No air

Every grain flies the same arc.

With nothing to slow it, kicked dust rises on a perfect parabola and lands at the same instant as the pebble next to it. No cloud. No drift. Just geometry.

02 The outpost

Built on a ridge that almost never sees night.

Shackleton Rim is one of the Moon’s peaks of near-eternal light. The Sun circles the horizon instead of setting, and our arrays turn to follow it. Two hundred metres away, the crater floor hasn’t seen sunlight in two billion years.

03 The seven days

At the pole, the Sun doesn’t set. It walks around you.

Scroll through the week. The Sun travels eighty-five degrees around the horizon, and every shadow on the rim turns with it like the hand of a clock.

  1. Day 1

    Touchdown

    Twelve minutes of powered descent from lunar orbit. Suit checks, then your first step onto the rim.

  2. Day 2

    The long walk

    A guided four-kilometre traverse along the ridge. You learn the lope, the slow bounding gait the Moon rewards.

  3. Day 3

    Into the dark

    A lamp-lit descent into a permanently shadowed crater, where water ice has waited since before the dinosaurs.

  4. Day 4

    Earth day

    Rest in the cabin. Call home, then watch the continents turn slowly in the window.

  5. Day 5

    De Gerlache rim

    Pressurised rover to the neighbouring crater. Lunch looking down four kilometres of cliff.

  6. Day 6

    Leave one thing

    Bury a sealed capsule in the regolith. We log the coordinates. It will outlive every city on Earth.

  7. Day 7

    Ascent

    Liftoff, rendezvous, three days drifting home. The prints stay.

Six weeks on Earth for seven days up there.
